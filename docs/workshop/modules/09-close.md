# Module 09 — Close: honest limits, and Monday

**10 minutes.** Compressible to 5. Never cut the honest-limits section — it's what makes
everything before it credible.

---

## Part 1 (4 min): the honest limits

Say all of these plainly. Adoption survives on credibility, and a room that's been told the
truth about a tool's edges trusts the rest of it more, not less.

**It can't make an agent honest.** Nothing prevents a dishonest ledger line. What the
structure does is make dishonesty *checkable* — a line that claims evidence names it, and a
named receipt can be verified. The trail raises the cost of a false claim; it doesn't make
one impossible. Don't oversell this.

**Outside version control, parts get weaker.** The trail still records what landed, but
nothing binds a line to a revision of the files it describes, and *what changed* has no
answer a machine can produce.

**It costs something.** One line per substantive prompt, plus the discipline to write it when
the work lands rather than later. That's real. The payoff arrives at the **seams** — session
death, a week away, a handover. If you never hit seams, you don't need this.

**Some of it is specific; some is universal.** The verbs are one implementation. The laws —
evidence over assertion, presence is not establishment, the trail wins, a warning isn't a
blocker — are about how you work, and they transfer to any tool or none. Be clear which is
which; don't let the room leave thinking the laws are proprietary.

## Part 2 (4 min): Monday

The mistake is adopting all of it. Adoption fails when the first step is large.

**Week one — one thing.** One project. One honest line per substantive prompt, when the work
lands. Nothing else. Not the gates, not delegation, not the continuation ritual.

**Week two — add the close.** End sessions deliberately instead of letting them stop. Then,
once, open a fresh session and try to continue from what you left. That single experiment
tells them more about their own notes than any advice will.

**Week three — add whichever failure is actually costing them.** Let the pain choose:

| the failure that hurts | the verb to add |
|---|---|
| Work lost at session death | `/sessionend` → `/notrest` continuation |
| "Done" you can't trust | `/fable-mode`, plus the gates |
| Delegation you can't audit | `/agentswarm` + `/spend` |
| No idea how the project got here | `/recap` |

## Part 3 (2 min): the last word

Three beats. Don't summarise the modules — they just did them.

**1 · The idea, once more.**

> Presence is not establishment. Installed is not in force. Configured is not verified. A
> test existing is not a test testing.

**2 · The one sentence everything compresses to.**

> **Write down what landed and how you know, the moment it lands.**

Everything in the harness is an elaboration of that sentence. Someone who takes only this
line and none of the verbs still leaves better off.

**3 · Where to go next.** They used ten verbs. There are thirty-two. The other twenty-two
will make sense once the habit is there — point at the cheatsheet and the tutorial, and stop
talking.

## Facilitator notes

- If you're compressing to five minutes, keep parts 1 and 3 and cut part 2 to the single
  "week one" line. People remember the honest limits and the last word.
- Expect *"how do I sell this to my team?"* The most effective answer isn't a feature list —
  it's to have them run module 08's kill-and-continue exercise in front of their team. The
  demonstration argues better than they can.
- **Do not end on a feature summary.** End on the one sentence. It's the only thing that
  survives the commute home.
