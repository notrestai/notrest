whichever fits the surface. All of it is honest about what the workshop does and doesn't
do — which matters, because the subject is evidence, and copy that oversells undercuts the

## Medium — for a signup page (~150 words)

You set up your instructions. You assume they're in force. Nothing errors, nothing logs, and
the session behaves exactly like one that's governed — right up until you discover it never
was.

That's one of four failures everyone hits eventually. The others: an agent that says *done*
with no receipt, a session that dies and takes everything it learned with it, and delegated
work that comes back unattributable and unpriced.

This workshop names all four, reproduces each one live, and then hands you the tool that
answers it — one verb at a time, with an overview and then a hands-on test drive. Every
exercise ends in something you can check: an exit code, a file that now exists, a count that
moved. Never "you should see something like this."

The finale is the test that matters: you deliberately kill a session, open a fresh one that
has never seen your work, and ask it to continue.

## Long — for a full event page (~350 words)

### The problem

Everyone who uses an AI coding agent long enough hits the same four failures, usually
without a name for them.

**The session that looked governed and wasn't.** You set up instructions and assume they're
in force. Nothing errors. Nothing logs. It looks exactly like a session that's working.

**"Done" that wasn't.** Fixed. Working. Deployed. A claim with no receipt — and you find out
two hours later, after you built on top of it.

**The context that evaporated.** The session ends, compacts, or dies. Everything it worked
out goes with it, and the next one re-derives it badly and charges you again.

**Delegation that lost the plot.** Work farmed out to sub-agents comes back unattributable,
unpriced, and impossible to audit. You solved your context problem and bought an
accountability problem.

### What we do about it

Three hours, hands-on throughout, walking one verb at a time. Each module opens with the
failure it exists to prevent — reproduced live, not described — and then you drive the tool
yourself.

There are thirty-one skills in the harness. You won't learn thirty-one things: they share a
single contract, taught in the first twenty minutes, after which each verb costs ten minutes
instead of twenty because you're only learning what differs.

The roster is deliberately weighted toward instruments that finish while you're watching.
Several cost zero model tokens and run in about a tenth of a second.

### The finale

You do real work, close the session properly, then open a brand-new one in the same folder
and — before telling it anything — ask what you were building. Either it answers, or you
learn exactly which line in your trail should have carried the fact it missed. Both outcomes
teach.

### The honest part

This is infrastructure for reliability, not magic. It can't make an agent honest — it makes
dishonesty *checkable*. It costs a line of writing per substantive prompt. The payoff arrives
at the seams: session death, a week away, a handover. If you never hit seams, you don't need
this. Most people hit them constantly and pay without noticing.

## Who it's for

- You already use Claude Code, and you've already been burned by it.
- You've lost work to a session that ended badly.
- You've acted on a "done" that turned out not to be.
- You delegate to sub-agents and can't audit what came back.

## Who it's not for

- People who have never used an AI coding agent. The failures won't land, and the workshop
  is built on recognition.
- Anyone looking for prompt-engineering technique. This is about record-keeping, evidence,
  and continuity — not about how to phrase a request.

## What you leave with

- An established project with a real ledger you wrote yourself
- A live window over your own estate
- One delegated lane with a receipt you can audit
- A session you deliberately killed and successfully continued from another
- Nine verbs you've actually driven, and the laws underneath them — which transfer to any
  tool, or none

## Prerequisites

Bring a laptop with Claude Code installed and working, `git` and `python3` on PATH, and a
scratch folder you don't mind writing files into. **Install before you arrive** — the first
twenty minutes are for the shared contract, not for setup.

## The one sentence

> **Write down what landed and how you know, the moment it lands.**

Everything in the harness is an elaboration of that. Anyone who takes only that line and none
of the verbs still leaves better off.
